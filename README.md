## The Core Features That Actually Matter

### Multi-Currency Business Accounts

Aspire lets you hold funds in multiple currencies—SGD, USD, EUR, GBP, and more depending on your location. You get local account details for USD (ACH), EUR (SEPA), and GBP (Faster Payments), which is huge if you're dealing with international clients or suppliers.

Why does this matter? Because when you have local account details, you avoid those annoying SWIFT fees that banks love to charge. Your US client can pay you via ACH transfer instead of an expensive international wire. It's like having bank accounts in multiple countries without actually having to open accounts in multiple countries.

### Corporate Cards With Real Benefits

This is where things get interesting. Aspire offers both virtual and physical corporate cards that you can issue to your team. But here's the kicker—they actually give you unlimited cashback.

Aspire provides 1% cashback on qualified spending for online marketing and SaaS expenses. For many modern businesses, that's where a huge chunk of spending goes. Google Ads, Facebook campaigns, Slack subscriptions, Notion, the list goes on. That 1% adds up fast.

You can create virtual cards instantly, set spending limits, block certain categories, and track every transaction in real-time. It's the kind of control that makes CFOs smile and employees less likely to accidentally spend the quarterly budget on "team building" expenses.

### Global Payments Made Simple

Sending money internationally has traditionally been about as fun as filing taxes. Aspire changes that equation by offering payments to 130+ countries in 30+ currencies with competitive FX rates.

The fee structure is straightforward: SWIFT transfers cost around $15-30 USD depending on the payment type, while local transfers in supported currencies are free. Compare that to traditional banks that charge you 3-5% in hidden FX markups plus fees, and you start to see why companies are making the switch.

### Expense Management That Doesn't Suck

Real-time expense tracking, automated approval workflows, budget controls—all the stuff you need to actually know where your money is going. You can set up approval chains, so that junior team member can't accidentally approve a $10,000 purchase without someone higher up the food chain signing off.

The system integrates with major accounting platforms like Xero, QuickBooks, and Oracle NetSuite. This means your bookkeeper won't hate you for creating a reconciliation nightmare.

### Yield Program: Make Your Money Work

One of the newer features is Aspire Yield, which lets eligible customers earn returns on their USD and SGD funds. You can earn up to 3.45% returns on USD with no minimums and no lock-ins. It's like a high-yield savings account but for your business funds, with next-business-day liquidity when you need to access the money.

Beyond the monthly fee, here are the main transaction costs:

**Incoming Transfers:**
- Local SGD transfers (FAST, PayNow, GIRO): Free
- Local USD (ACH): Free
- SWIFT transfers with FX conversion: $8 USD
- SWIFT transfers (SGD account): $35 SGD

**Outgoing Transfers:**
- Local transfers in supported currencies: Free
- SWIFT SHA (shared fees): $15 USD
- SWIFT OUR (all fees paid by sender): $30 USD

**Card Fees:**
- Virtual cards: Free
- Physical cards: Varies by location
- ATM withdrawals: $5 SGD + bank processing fees
- Daily card limit: $500 USD equivalent

**Other Fees:**
- No setup fees
- No minimum balance requirements
- No monthly maintenance fees on base account
- FX fees at competitive mid-market rates (typically better than banks)

The actual cost depends heavily on your usage. If you're mainly doing domestic transfers and using the cards for cashback categories, you might spend next to nothing. If you're moving money internationally every week, those SWIFT fees add up—but they're still usually cheaper than traditional banks.
